(Q.No. 1)

1.What is an affray under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and what is the punishment provided for it?[5]
Ans. Two or more persons who, by fighting in a public place, disturb the public peace commit an affray (Section 194(1)). The fighting must be mutual and in a public place, so a one-sided assault, or a fight inside a private house, is not an affray. It is punishable with imprisonment of either description up to one month, or fine up to one thousand rupees, or both (Section 194(2)).

(Q.No. 2)

2.What is the offence of personating a public servant under the Indian Penal Code, 1860, and what is the punishment provided for it?[5]
Ans. The offence lies in assuming the character of an office and then acting in it. Whoever pretends to hold a particular office as a public servant, knowing he does not, or falsely personates another holding it, and in that assumed character does or attempts any act under colour of such office, is punishable with imprisonment up to two years, or fine, or both (Section 170).

(Q.No. 3)

3.Explain the offence of an act endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. State the punishment provided for it, and explain the Explanation appended to that section.[10]
Ans. The offence is committed where a person, purposely or knowingly, by words spoken or written, by signs, by visible representation, by electronic communication or by use of financial means, excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities, or encourages feelings of separatist activities, or endangers the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India, or indulges in or commits any such act (Section 152). The mental element is express, so careless speech falls outside it. The punishment is imprisonment for life, or up to seven years, and fine.
The Explanation keeps lawful criticism outside the section: comments expressing disapprobation of the measures, or administrative or other action, of the Government, with a view to obtaining their alteration by lawful means, are not an offence, provided they do not excite the activities described. It replaces sedition under section 124A of the Indian Penal Code, 1860, which is not re-enacted.

(Q.No. 4)

4.Explain the offences under sections 186, 188 and 189 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860, relating to obstruction of a public servant in the discharge of his public functions, disobedience to an order duly promulgated by a public servant, and threat of injury to a public servant. State the punishment in each case and point out the difference between them.[10]
Ans. All three shield the public servant at different stages. Voluntarily obstructing a public servant in his public functions draws three months, or fine up to five hundred rupees, or both (Section 186). Disobeying an order duly promulgated by a public servant is graded: where it causes or tends to cause obstruction, annoyance or injury to a person lawfully employed, simple imprisonment up to one month, or fine up to two hundred rupees; and where it endangers human life, health or safety, or causes a riot or affray, six months, or fine up to one thousand rupees (Section 188). Threatening a public servant with injury to induce him to do or forbear any act connected with his functions draws two years, or fine, or both (Section 189).
The difference is the mode: 186 obstructs an act being done, 188 defies an order already made, and 189 intimidates the officer himself.

(Q.No. 5)

5.“An assembly of five or more persons is designated an ‘unlawful assembly’, if the common object of the persons composing that assembly is to overawe by criminal force, or show of criminal force, the Central Government or any State Government or Parliament or the Legislature of a State, or any public servant in the exercise of the lawful power of such public servant.”
Comment on the above statement and explain when an unlawful assembly becomes rioting under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Explain also the liability of every member of an unlawful assembly for an offence committed in prosecution of the common object, and the liability of the owner or occupier of land on which an unlawful assembly is held. Discuss with the help of decisions of the Supreme Court.[20]
Ans. An assembly of five or more is unlawful if its members’ common object is one of the five named in Section 189(1) — to overawe the Government or a public servant by criminal force; to resist the execution of any law or legal process; to commit mischief, criminal trespass or any other offence; to take possession of property or deprive a person of an incorporeal right; or to compel a person to do what he is not legally bound to do. An assembly lawful at its inception may become unlawful afterwards. Mere membership draws six months, or fine, or both; joining or continuing after a command to disperse, and membership while armed with a deadly weapon, each draw two years.
It becomes rioting the moment force or violence is used by the assembly or any member in prosecution of that object; every member is then guilty of rioting, punishable with two years, and five years if armed with a deadly weapon (Section 191).
Where an offence is committed by any member in prosecution of the common object, or such as the members knew to be likely, every member at that time is guilty of it (Section 190). The object must be shared by five or more; mere presence does not attract it. Masalti v. State of U.P. requires a consistent number of witnesses, and Mizaji v. State of U.P. explains the second, ‘knew to be likely’, limb.
The owner or occupier of the land, and anyone claiming an interest, is liable to fine up to one thousand rupees if, knowing or having reason to believe a riot or unlawful assembly is or is likely to be committed there, he fails to give the earliest notice to the nearest police station and to use all lawful means to suppress it (Section 193).

(Q.No. 6)

6.“Whoever by words, either spoken or written, or by signs or by visible representations or otherwise, promotes or attempts to promote, on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, caste or community or any other ground whatsoever, disharmony or feelings of enmity, hatred or ill-will between different religious, racial, language or regional groups or castes or communities, shall be punished.”
Comment on the above statement with reference to section 153A of the Indian Penal Code, 1860. Explain the aggravated form of the offence where it is committed in a place of worship, and distinguish it from the offence of imputations and assertions prejudicial to national integration under section 153B. Discuss with the help of decisions of the Supreme Court.[20]
Ans. Three things are punished: promoting or attempting to promote, by words spoken or written, by signs or visible representations or otherwise, disharmony or feelings of enmity, hatred or ill-will between such groups, on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, caste or any other ground; committing an act prejudicial to the maintenance of harmony which is likely to disturb public tranquillity; and organising or participating in any activity intending participants to use or be trained to use criminal force against such a group. It carries three years, or fine, or both (Section 153A(1)).
It is aggravated where committed in a place of worship, or in any assembly engaged in religious worship or ceremonies, and then carries five years and fine (Section 153A(2)).
Two propositions are settled. At least two groups are needed: words aimed at a single community, or at the Government, do not attract it — Bilal Ahmed Kaloo v. State of A.P. Mens rea is essential, the effect of the words being judged by the standard of reasonable, strong-minded persons — Ramesh v. Union of India; casual slogans raised by a few, drawing no response, did not attract it in Balwant Singh v. State of Punjab.
The companion provision is narrower and protects national integration. It punishes imputations that a class cannot, by reason of membership of such a group, bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution or uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India; assertions that such a class be denied their rights as citizens; and assertions about its obligations likely to cause disharmony. It carries three years, or fine, or both, rising to five years and fine in a place of worship (Section 153B).
The distinction is of object: 153A guards tranquillity between groups, 153B a class against having its loyalty impugned.
